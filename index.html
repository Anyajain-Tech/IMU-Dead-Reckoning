How It Works

Follow one signal from the phone in your pocket to a position on the map. Scroll to send it down the line.

01
STAGE 01 / 06 · SMARTPHONE IMU

Motion starts in your phone

The phone's accelerometer and gyroscope stream raw motion: acceleration on three axes and rotation rate. This is the only sensor input DeadReck needs.

IN phone motionOUT raw a(t), ω(t)
02
STAGE 02 / 06 · FILTERING

Noise is filtered out

Road vibration and sensor jitter are removed, so only the real motion of the vehicle continues down the line.

IN raw a(t), ω(t)OUT smooth a(t), ω(t)
03
STAGE 03 / 06 · CALIBRATION

Sensors are calibrated

Sensor bias and orientation offsets are estimated and removed automatically, so every reading starts from a clean zero.

IN smooth a(t), ω(t)OUT zero-bias a(t), ω(t)
04
STAGE 04 / 06 · AI SPEED

AI predicts the speed

An LSTM network reads short windows of clean IMU data and predicts vehicle speed. No speedometer and no GPS.

IN IMU windowsOUT speed v
05
STAGE 05 / 06 · PATH

Speed and heading become a path

Each step moves the position by speed × time along the heading. Step after step, that builds the vehicle's path.

IN speed v, heading θOUT path x, y
06
STAGE 06 / 06 · MAP FUSION

The path merges with GNSS on the map

When GNSS is available, its fixes anchor the path and cancel accumulated drift. When GNSS drops, dead reckoning carries on. The result is one continuous track on the map.

IN DR path + GNSS fixesOUT fused track

Dead Reckoning Explained

Navigate without satellites.

01

What is Dead Reckoning?

Dead reckoning estimates your current position from a known starting point, plus how fast and in which direction you have moved. It needs no external signal, which is why it works during GPS outages.

The drawback is drift: small errors accumulate over time.

02

Why IMU + AI?

A phone's accelerometer and gyroscope measure motion, but integrating acceleration directly drifts within seconds. A LSTM learns the relationship between IMU patterns and real speed, giving stable estimates over longer windows.

03

Speed

The model outputs speed from short sliding windows of IMU data. Speed alone cannot give position; it must be combined with heading and integrated over time.

04

Heading

Heading is the direction of travel, in degrees clockwise from North. It is estimated from gyroscope rates and orientation, with bias correction to limit drift.

05

Position Update

Each step moves the position by distance = speed × Δt along the current heading. Vehicle constraints (no sideways or vertical motion) keep the estimate realistic.
